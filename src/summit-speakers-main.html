Content guidelines

These keep the weekend welcoming and keep all of us on the right side of the line between teaching about money and giving personal financial advice. Unless you're a licensed professional speaking in that role, telling someone what to do with their own money can create legal risk for you and for me, and it can steer an attendee wrong, because you don't know their whole situation. Teaching how money works is exactly what this weekend is for.

  • Teach, don't advise

    Share how things work and what the options are, and what you've learned. Please don't tell attendees what to do with their own money, like which account to open or what to invest in.

  • Save the sales pitch

    Please don't recommend or pitch specific retirement-account or investment products, and keep paid offers out of your session. Attendees trust a speaker more when the session is pure value. Welcome them into your world first with a freebie or a follow, then build the relationship from there.

  • No promised results

    Your story and real examples are great! Please don't promise attendees a specific income, savings number or result, including in your session title.

  • Every path is welcome

    Most of our attendees are current classroom teachers, and we welcome educators of every kind: specialists, support staff, administrators, people moving into new roles, and people who've already left the classroom. Some love teaching and plan to stay for decades. Please skip "quit teaching" framing, and make what you teach useful whichever path someone is on.
